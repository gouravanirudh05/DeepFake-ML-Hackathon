Deepfake Detection: Report

Deepfake ML Hackathon, IIIT Bangalore. Trained only on the provided 7,000 training images (no external datasets).

1. Architecture

A single DINOv2 ViT-L/14 (with 4 register tokens) foundation backbone, kept frozen and adapted with parameter-efficient LoRA, run at 224×224. It was the best of the five backbones we compared (CLIP, DINOv2, DINOv3, PE-Core; see table). Following RINE (ECCV 2024), the detector reads the CLS token after blocks 3, 7, 11, 15, 19 and 23 (as listed in the training config as auto), applies a LayerNorm to each, concatenates them and feeds a dropout + linear head that outputs one logit. Mid-level layers keep the low-level blending/texture traces that the last layer discards. LoRA (rank 16, alpha 32) is applied to all linear layers; the backbone stays frozen. After training the adapters are merged into the weights, so inference is a plain timm ViT. The final score is the logit averaged over the image and its horizontal flip (TTA), passed through a sigmoid; this is the confidence = P(Fake). The label uses a threshold of 0.5433, chosen by Youden's J on out-of-fold predictions only, never on test data. Pretrained backbone checkpoints (public foundation models) were used for initialisation; no extra datasets were used.

2. Training and augmentations

  • Loss: BCE with label smoothing 0.05. AdamW (lr 3e-4 LoRA, 1e-3 head, wd 0.05), cosine schedule with 5% warmup, 5 epochs, batch 32, fp16 autocast, gradient checkpointing.
  • Label-noise handling: a small-loss filter ignores the 8% highest-loss samples in each batch (ramped in over the first epoch). The data audit suggested about 8% confidently wrong labels.
  • Geometric: random horizontal flip (p=0.5), mild random-resized crop (scale 0.85–1.0, p=0.5).
  • Robustness degradations (random subset, random order): JPEG q 30–95, Gaussian blur σ 0.2–2, Gaussian noise σ 2–15, down/up-scaling 0.35–0.9, brightness/contrast/saturation jitter, grayscale, cutout 10–30%. These cover the likely hidden test variants.
  • Self-blended pseudo-fakes (SBI) are implemented and were tested, but are off in the submitted model (no gain in our CLIP experiments).

3. Validation strategy

A data audit found near-duplicates by perceptual hash (295 near-duplicate pairs, 53 real/fake pairs of the same source photo). Duplicates were grouped, and a StratifiedGroupKFold (5 folds) keeps every group inside one fold, which prevents leakage between train and validation. All models were trained on folds 1–4 and validated on fold 0 (1,412 images). Besides clean AUC, each model is scored on 10 degraded variants (JPEG, blur, noise, downscale, colour, grayscale, cutout and a combined one) to measure robustness.

Submitted model, out-of-fold: AUC 0.915, accuracy 0.888, F1 0.890 at the chosen threshold. Earlier CLIP ViT-L/14 LoRA runs reached 0.911–0.917 on the same fold; their weights were not saved, so they could not be submitted. Ensembling the backbones gave at most +0.0006 AUC, so we submit the single best model.

4. Limitations

The submitted model was trained on 80% of the data (fold 0 held out) for 5 epochs, so test performance should be similar to the 0.91–0.92 validation AUC. Differences between single models are within the noise of one fold (about ±0.01). Inference runs one ViT-L with flip TTA and is much faster on GPU than on CPU. inference_script.py is self-contained and needs no downloads; with a fixed device it reproduces predictions.csv.
